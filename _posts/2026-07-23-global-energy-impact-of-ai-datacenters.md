# The energy cost of AI: From your screen to orbital datacenters

After a discussion with my colleagues about my daily AI use, I decided to dig into the subject of my AI footprint. In fact, I use AI for everything, from being my personal gym coach to explaining the impact of vaccination on my health. When I have a question in mind, my reflex now is to ask Gemini or Copilot. So, I had a discussion with Gemini and Copilot, and here is a summary of what I found out (with sources included at the end).

## 1. AI prompts: small individual impact

Studies show that AI inference consumes very little energy individually but demand is rising fast as AI adoption accelerates.
Global datacenter electricity demand could reach 1,050 TWh (almost the annual electricity consumption of Japan per year) by 2026, making datacenters one of the world’s top energy consumers. 

AI training is far more energy intensive: training large models like GPT‑4 can consume 50 GWh annually (equivalent to the electricity use of 5,000 households). 

## 2. Social media: lower per‑action impact

Social media’s environmental impact comes generally from continuous video streaming, recommendation algorithms, billions of daily active users, high‑bandwidth content delivery.

Analyses show that a single AI prompt is equivalent a few seconds of video streaming (100 prompts of simple text (25 Wh) is equivalent 5/15min of scrollings), but social media’s aggregate footprint is enormous because of billions of users and constant usage.

Social platforms also create indirect climate impacts by shaping consumption behaviour and attention patterns, which can increase emissions and weaken climate action.

## 3. Global datacenter expansion

The world is entering a multi‑trillion‑dollar datacenter expansion cycle driven by AI, cloud, and digital services. Forecasts show global datacenter capacity rising to 120-220 GW by 2030 (near three times the French electric power capacity, HUGE).

Consequences will be multiple with electricity demand doubling by 2030, local grid congestion and rising electricity prices in datacenter‑dense regions, water consumption for cooling and increased e‑waste from specialized hardware and community opposition to new datacenter construction due to land, water, and noise concerns.

This expansion is reshaping real estate markets, energy policy, and national infrastructure planning.

## 4. AI Energy Paradox: Consumption vs. Efficiency

AI uses a lot of power, but it’s also helping us save energy in big ways.

Engineers are making AI smarter and lighter so it needs far less power to run. At the same time, data centers are switching to clean solar or wind energy and using advanced liquid cooling to stay efficient.

People are using AI to fix other big energy problems like making power grids smarter, reducing factory waste, and keeping buildings efficient.

This creates an interesting paradox: AI consumes a lot of electricity, but it might also be the tool that helps us save the most energy in the long run.

## 5. Consequences of datacenters spreading across many countries

As datacenters multiply across the world, countries are facing a series of increasingly visible pressures. 

In regions with dense clusters such as the US East Coast, Ireland, and the Netherlands, local electrical grids are becoming congested, forcing expensive upgrades to transmission lines and sparking political debates over who should pay for new generation capacity. 

Water scarcity is another growing issue: large cooling systems can consume millions of liters per day, creating tension in drought‑prone areas like Arizona or southern Spain. The surge in datacenter construction is also reshaping real‑estate markets, driving up land prices near fiber backbones and renewable‑energy hubs,.

At the local level, communities often push back against new datacenter projects due to noise, water use, land consumption, and the perception that these facilities bring few direct benefits to residents.

## 6. Moving AI datacenters into space

Elon Musk, SpaceX, and xAI are pushing a radical idea: orbital AI datacenters. Competitors like Blue Origin, Nvidia, Google, and China are pursuing similar concepts.

### Why space?

Space offers constant solar power, no need for water‑intensive cooling, no land or local grid constraints and potentially lower long‑term costs if launch prices fall dramatically.

SpaceX is ideally positioned because it already operates with Starlink (satellite internet), Starship (rocket), thousands of satellites and xAI models.

### The Starmind project and AI1, the first orbital datacenter

SpaceX is recruiting engineers for Starmind, a constellation of satellites designed to run AI inference in orbit. Each satellite acts like one rack of an AI datacenter, powered by solar arrays and cooled via radiative panels in space .

SpaceX’s AI1 satellite is a solar‑powered supercomputer in low‑Earth orbit. Company has filed for a constellation of up to one million orbital datacenter satellites, targeting 1 terawatt of space‑based AI compute by 2027–2028 .
